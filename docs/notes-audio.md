## How it is made

Procedural synthesis on the CPU, numpy only (no scipy, no models, no GPU): `tools/.venv` + `soundfile` for OGG.
The three room loops are processed recordings (`rec.py`, below). Scripts cap numpy at 4 threads.

```sh
nice -n 15 tools/.venv/bin/python audio-src/sfx/synth.py              # all ids (~20 s), or: synth.py knock coin
nice -n 15 tools/.venv/bin/python audio-src/sfx/synth.py --wav DIR    # also write 16-bit WAV masters to DIR
tools/.venv/bin/python audio-src/sfx/check.py                         # QA → audio-src/sfx/report.md, exit 1 on problems
```

- `audio-src/sfx/dsp.py` primitives: exact RBJ biquad responses applied in the frequency domain (zero-padded for
  one-shots, circular for loops), STFT time-varying filters, modal synthesis (struck wood, glass, coins, bells,
  steel), band-limited additive harmonic sources through Klatt-style formant cascades (dogs, voices, brass, reeds,
  horn), synthetic room impulse responses, loudness meters.
- Generators: `gen_table.py` (cards, taps, pencil, paper, stamp, chess, UI, sudoku), `gen_inn.py` (glasses, bottle,
  pour, gulp, burp, chair, coins, till, murmur), `gen_road.py` (engine model, van, bike, door, horn, fuel, ratchet),
  `gen_nature.py` (dogs, church bell, rain, crickets), `gen_stings.py` (accordion, brass, fanfare).
- `synth.py` holds the registry (id → category, generator, 1–3 variant parameter sets, description) and masters:
  DC-block → trim with 8 ms silent pre-roll and a 20 ms fade (one-shots) → loudness normalisation → OGG Vorbis.
- **Provenance (§0.12):** per file the generator function, seed (`crc32(id) % 10000 * 10 + variant` unless given),
  parameters, category, target, measured loudness, gain, peaks, sample count and SHA-256 of the file. "Model" is
  always none. Renders are **byte-reproducible**: libsndfile writes a random Ogg stream serial, so `synth.py`
  rewrites it to `crc32(filename)` and recomputes the page checksums.

## Format decisions

- 44.1 kHz, OGG Vorbis (quality 0.4–0.6 by category). Mono, except where stereo helps: the three ambience loops,
  `shuffle` (the two halves of the riffle), `cheers`, `church-bell`, the three stings.
- **Levels are baked into the files**; `"gain": 0` everywhere in `sfx.json` (free for mixing in code).
- Variants are picked at random by `Sfx.play`, so variants of one id differ only in detail (pitch, timing, glass
  pair), never in meaning. Feedback sounds (`sudoku-right/-wrong`, `ui-*`) have near-identical variants.
- Loops carry `loopStart: 0` and `loopEnd` = exact length; Chromium decodes all of them sample-exact (checked).
- Loop lengths: synthetic loops 6–12 s, recorded room loops 30 s (so the real voices do not repeat audibly);
  `check.py` enforces 6–12 s and 20–40 s respectively.

## Loudness targets (nothing should startle)

One-shots: maximum K-weighted loudness over a sliding 200 ms window ("M200max", LUFS-ish). Loops: gated integrated
loudness. Peaks: true-peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP before encoding, decoded sample peak ≤ −1 dBFS (verified).

| category | target | ids |
|---|---:|---|
| ui | −26 | `ui-click`, `ui-back`, `ui-open`, `card-raise` |
| feedback | −24 | `sudoku-right`, `sudoku-wrong` |
| card, table | −23 | cards, `knock`, `bid`, `pass`, `list-write`, `pencil`, `stamp`, `page-turn`, chess |
| drink | −24 | `glass-clink`, `bottle-open`, `pour`, `gulp`, `cheers` |
| mild | −27 | `burp` |
| foley | −22 | `chair`, `coin`, `till`, `van-door`, `fuel`, `wrench` |
| vehicle | −21 | `van-start`, `bike-kick` (`van-horn` −23, `bike-rev` −22 via per-id trim) |
| dog | −22.5 | the four dog sounds |
| bell | −23 | `church-bell` (distant) |
| sting | −18 | `win-sting`, `lose-sting`, `trophy-fanfare` |
| room loops (recorded) | −36 integrated | `inn-murmur`, `beergarden`; `inn-murmur-busy` −35 (a touch livelier) |
| ambience loops | −30 integrated | `rain`, `crickets` (beds under the music) |
| engine loops | −26 integrated | `van-drive`, `bike-idle` |

The music is to arrive at −19 LUFS; with the default bus volumes (music 0.7, sfx 0.8) cards and taps sit just
under the music, stings a little above, ambience well below.

## Sound design decisions

- **Drinking is a quiet joke** (user decision 2026-10-04): `pour`, `gulp` and `bottle-open` are short, soft and
  dry. `burp` is deliberately tiny: variant 1 a muffled closed-mouth "örp" (0.17 s), variant 2 a polite throat
  clear ("hm-hm"). Both sit 3 dB under the other drink sounds.
- **Cards on felt:** a soft "tock" (card + table body mode around 230–290 Hz) with a muted slap and an air puff, no
  bright crack. `bid` = two fingertip taps, `pass` = a soft thumb flick over the fanned cards; both are quiet
  enough to sit under a speech bubble.
- **Laptop speakers and older ears:** everything carries its identity between 200 Hz and 4 kHz (no reliance on deep
  bass or on >6 kHz shimmer); crickets sit at 3.2–4.4 kHz, the deep woof has formants at 500–1300 Hz.
- **Engines** are firing-event models (integrated 4-stroke phase, per-cylinder offsets, pulse sharpness with
  throttle, exhaust resonances): the van is an even-firing four, the motorcycle a 270° twin (uneven
  "potato-potato" idle). Loops use whole engine cycles and circular filtering: seamless by construction.
- **Loops:** everything in `rain`, `crickets`, `van-drive`, `bike-idle` is rendered circularly (events wrap,
  filters and reverb are circular, cricket rhythms divide the loop). The recorded room loops are cut 3 s longer
  and equal-power crossfaded into their start. Seam scores 0.1–0.5 (second-difference energy on the seam vs. the
  99.9th percentile of the loop; a click would score far above 1; `check.py` fails above 1.2).
- The retired synthetic babble (`gen_inn.inn_murmur`, twelve formant-synthesised speakers) stays in the code for
  reference but is no longer in the registry.
- **Stings:** additive instruments, not samples: accordion = three detuned reeds (musette ±6–7 cents) with a
  bellows envelope and an oom-pah left hand; brass = harmonics that brighten with loudness, a small pitch scoop,
  delayed vibrato, two players per part. `lose-sting` is a muted trombone "wah wah wah waaah" with a comic droop.

## Recorded room loops (Freesound)

Requirements from the user/lead: calm room tone of a Gasthaus, distant indistinct talk (German or unintelligible,
never English in front), soft clinks, no music/TV, no laughter bursts or shouting, quiet (about −36 LUFS).

**Search.** Freesound search pages (no API key needed) for restaurant/pub/Gasthaus/Wirtshaus/Kneipe/beer hall/
Biergarten/beer garden/Oktoberfest/Festzelt/terrace, duration ≥ 30 s; only **CC0** or **CC BY** kept (CC BY-NC,
Sampling+ and unclear licences dropped). 18 candidates were downloaded as HQ previews (Freesound's originals need a
login; previews carry the same licence) and screened by measurement, since this workstream cannot listen:
loudness bursts over 400 ms windows (laughs, shouts), modulation depth of the 300–3000 Hz envelope at 2–8 Hz (a
single close talker scores high, diffuse babble low), persistent narrow spectral peaks (music, TV, hum; calibrated
on a restaurant recording with classical music), clipping, and spectrograms by eye (music shows as note lines).

| id | source | licence | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `inn-murmur` | BonnyOrbit, „German restaurant with a crowd“ (Berlin), 18–51 s | CC0 | German voices, plates and glasses, no music, no bursts in the window, diffuse (mod 0.18) |
| `inn-murmur-busy` | Garuda1982, „big crowd in a hall talking“ (Gewandhaus Leipzig), 35–68 s | CC BY 4.0 | the most diffuse crowd (mod 0.09), steady, big-room sound like a tent, no music |
| `beergarden` | Garuda1982, „Restaurant outside by the river …“ (Leipzig, evening), 35–68 s | CC BY 4.0 | distant voices, birds at 1.5–5 kHz, river air; no music |

Rejected: Oktoberfest tents (Westmed, CC0) because a band/singing shows as tonal lines; two beer gardens that clip
(Sun_Rhythms, bvaudio); Bremen Biergarten and Würzburg restaurant (frequent bursts, close talkers); English pubs
(language in front); the Dresden "Scheune" restaurant (CC0, very even, no music) is the reserve for `inn-murmur`.

**Processing** (`audio-src/sfx/rec.py`, registry entries in `synth.py`): previews cached in the git-ignored
`audio-src/logs/freesound/` and pinned by SHA-256 → 33 s excerpt → slow leveller (above median + 3 dB, 3:1, 120 ms
attack / 600 ms release) → high-pass 110 Hz (beer garden 160 Hz) → low-pass 2.2 kHz, 12 dB/oct (busy 2.4 kHz; beer
garden 5.5 kHz so the birds survive), presence shelf −3 dB at 1.2 kHz → stereo width 0.5 (beer garden 0.6) → 3 s
equal-power loop crossfade → room target loudness → OGG Vorbis (quality 0.45, about 300–400 kB each). The
low-pass moves the spectral centroid of the inn room from 1.8 kHz to 0.8 kHz: the talk is warm murmur, not words.

## Music: skipped on purpose

The brief allowed placeholder loops only if they sound genuinely pleasant. This workstream cannot listen, and
procedural waltz/accordion/guitar music from numpy almost always ends up sounding like General MIDI. A cheesy
placeholder would be worse than silence for the first impression, so no music was rendered. The accordion and brass
voices in `gen_stings.py` could be reused for placeholders later if the user wants to audition some.

## What to listen to first (weakest by design risk)

Verified only by measurement and spectrograms, not by ear. In order of risk: the dogs (`dog-whine`, `dog-bark-*`), `burp` and `sudoku-wrong` (synthetic voice/hum),
`pour`/`gulp` (liquids), `bike-kick`/`bike-rev`/`van-start` (engine character), the stings (may sound
keyboard-like). Impacts are the safest: `knock`, `chess-*`, `ui-*`, `glass-clink`, `coin`, `till`, `wrench`,
`church-bell`. Any of them can be re-tuned by editing its parameters in `SOUNDS` (synth.py) and re-rendering one id.
